Shabbat Policy

HASC Center recognizes and respects the religious beliefs and practices of both our employees and residents in the residential program. This policy outlines the expectations for staff members working during Shabbat to ensure a respectful and supportive environment for those observing the holy day.

It is important to remember that while respecting Shabbat observance is crucial, ensuring the safety and well-being of residents remains the top priority. Striking a balance between these considerations is vital in creating a harmonious and supportive environment for all.

Scope of the Policy

This policy applies to all staff members employed in the HASC Center residential program, regardless of their personal religious beliefs. It applies to all work-related activities during Shabbat and Jewish holidays.

Shabbos Environment and DSP Expectations

Creating the "Avira" (Atmosphere) of Shabbos

The role of a Shabbos DSP extends far beyond simply completing a checklist of scheduled activities. While facilitating the logistical elements of the day—such as accompanying individuals to shul and managing the seudos—is essential, our primary expectation is that staff actively cultivate the avira of Shabbos. DSPs are responsible for fostering a warm, joyful, and restful environment that truly elevates the day for the individuals we support.

Dress Code and Presentation

To help set this tone, staff attire must reflect the dignity and special nature of Shabbos. While formal Shabbos clothing (such as a full suit or formal dress) is not strictly required, your presentation must demonstrate respect for the Shabbos environment.

  • Acceptable attire: Elevated casual or business casual wear (e.g., button-down shirts, appropriate slacks, modest skirts/dresses).
  • Unacceptable attire: Sweatpants, t-shirts, hoodies, athletic wear, or overly casual clothing.

Baseline Observance and Halachic Standards

All staff on duty must maintain strict adherence to standard Shabbos observances. This serves as the foundation for the environment we provide. This baseline includes, but is not limited to:

  • Complete abstention from personal cell phone use while in the presence of individuals or in common areas.
  • Strict adherence to the laws of Shabbos regarding electronics (e.g., not turning lights on or off, not operating appliances).
  • Active and respectful participation in the essential framework of the day, including tefillah and seudos.

Electronic Device Usage Around Individuals

  • Prohibited: The use of personal electronic devices, including but not limited to cell phones, smartwatches, tablets, and laptops, is strictly prohibited while on duty during Shabbat and Holidays. This includes making or receiving calls, texting, accessing social media, playing games, taking pictures, or recording audio and video.
  • Exceptions: Exceptions may be granted for emergency or medical situations. Supervisors will establish and communicate clear protocols for emergency communication during Shabbat in case of any urgent situations.
  • Respectful Behavior: Staff members are expected to refrain from any public conduct that may be perceived as disrespectful or disruptive to residents' Shabbat and Holiday observance.

Sleeping While on Shift

  • Due to the critical nature of 24/7 care provided in the residential program, sleeping during work hours is not permitted.
  • If you are working as an overnight awake staff, you must remain awake during the entire time of your shift.
  • If you are working as an overnight sleep staff, you may sleep from 11:00 am to 7:00 am only.
  • Staff members responsible for direct care of residents must remain alert and awake throughout their entire shift to ensure the safety and well-being of individuals entrusted to their care. Sleeping during shift is considered neglect.
  • Strategies for managing productivity during Shabbat shifts can be discussed with supervisors, such as rotating assignments or introduction of engaging activities and community integration.

Training and Education

Your supervisor will provide on the job training and education for all residential program staff regarding the principles and practices of Shabbat observance. This will help staff members understand the significance of the holy day and ensure they fulfill their work duties in a respectful and sensitive manner.

If you would like additional training on Shabbat practices and observances, please be in touch with your supervisor.

Rabbi Salgo, HASC Center's Chaplain is available to answer specific questions or concerns at 646-285-5337.

Examples of some things you can't do on Shabbat

Remember: This is a basic list and does not cover all aspects of Shabbat observance. For detailed guidance, please discuss with your manager.

General Restrictions

  • No cooking or baking: Prepare and cook all meals before Shabbat and keep them warm (e.g., crock pots, insulated containers, heat them up on the hot plate).
  • No electricity: Turning on lights, appliances, or electronics is prohibited. This includes charging devices, using TVs, radios, or computers.
  • Candles: Make sure to light the candles before Shabbat starts, as lighting them during Shabbat is prohibited.
  • No showering or bathing, unless it is medically required.
  • Medical needs: Certain medical equipment and procedures may be used if deemed necessary under the advice of their doctor along with consultation of a Rabbi.

Violation of Policy

Violation of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.

Contact

For any questions or concerns regarding this policy, please contact the Human Resources department.

Enunciators, locks with key pads /codes, GPS devices

  • Must be on at all times and is not allowed to be disabled
  • . If an individual or shabbos staff needs to go out a non-Jewish staff member should be designated before shabbos to turn off and back on the lock throughout Sabbath.
  • Staff are allowed to attach the GPS to the individuals article of clothing

Before Shabbos

  • Make sure that your crock pot with your cholent is set up with a shabbos timer that is set to turn off after lunch
  • Make sure that any hot plate is set up with a shabbos timer that is set to turn off after Friday night meal

Shabbos- Friday Night

  • All food that is going to be used for dinner should be placed onto the hot plate or blech before shabbos.
  • Once the food is removed from the hot plate or blech food is not allowed to be returned to the hot plate or blech for rewarming
  • If you're using a hot plate, it must be on a shabbos timer that shuts off later that night
  • If you are warming up food in the oven -- the oven must be turned off before Shabbos

Shabbos -- Day

  • No Hot Plate or Blech may be used for warming food on Shabbos day the hot cholent form the crock pot is served.
  • Managers should ensure that there are cold cuts and chicken cutlets in the home
  • Staff should take the chicken cutlets out in the morning so they should be room temperature.

Shalash sehudas

  • Will consist of foods items that can be eaten at room temperature or cold
  • Managers should ensure there is tuna fish, egg salad, vegetables available

Kashrus Rules

Cooking- has been defined as anything that can not be eaten in its raw state such as eggs)

  • Any individual in the residence may light a fire for cooking
  • Authorized Staff are permitted to assist any individual with the cooking process but may not do the cooking themselves.
  • Staff are not permitted to cook for themselves

Rewarming- has been defined as anything that has been fully cooked
